# Mini leader arm (teleoperation), v1 draft

![leader arm](preview.png)

A 0.4-scale copy of the SCARA follower arm, one per follower arm (build a pair). You move it by hand and the
follower copies its joint angles 1:1. It has the same 5 joints in the same order (shoulder pan, elbow pan,
wrist pan, wrist pitch, wrist roll) plus a gripper trigger, a thumbstick for the lift and a clutch button.

- Links 52 mm (follower: 130 mm). The grip is full size (100 × 30 × 34 mm), because it's what you hold.
- Every joint reads its angle with an **AS5600 magnetic encoder**: no contact, no wear, no friction from the sensor.
- The joints hold their pose by friction (an adjustable pad), and the follower only moves while you hold the
  **clutch button**.
- Printed volume about 345 cm³ per leader (≈200–230 g at 3 walls / 20 % infill), of which the base is about
  half. The part you move is roughly 170 g including bearings, bolts and boards.

Generated by `mobile_manipulator_description/generator/leader.py` (geometry), `leader_check.py` (clashes and
joint ranges) and `leader_export.py` (these files).

## Before you print: check the encoder board

The design uses the Robokits RKI-6142 AS5600 module as listed (about **23 × 23 mm**, with a Ø6 × 2.5 mm
diametric magnet). The seller doesn't publish a drawing, so **measure one board when it arrives** (width ×
length × thickness, height of the chip, where the chip sits, magnet Ø × thickness) and send the numbers: they
are parameters (`BOARD`, `MAG` in `leader.py`), and a re-export takes a minute.

Also check that each magnet is **diametrically** magnetised: spin it over the chip, and the reading should go
smoothly once round from 0 to 4095.

## How a joint goes together (pan joints and pitch)

1. Press a 685ZZ into each prong of the moving fork (the upper one from below, the lower one from above).
2. Put the fork over the fixed hub, slide the M5 × 30 up from below with a **spacer ring** under its head,
   then a spacer ring on top, then the nyloc nut. Tighten until there's no play but the joint still turns freely.
   The bolt, spacers and nut stay fixed with the hub; the fork turns on the bearings.
3. Glue the magnet into the **magnet cap** and press the cap onto the nut.
4. Drop the AS5600 board into the housing on top of the fork, **chip facing down** (towards the magnet), pins
   towards the wire slot. Screw on the lid with 2 × M2.
5. Put a felt or PTFE pad in the pocket under the upper prong and turn the M3 set screw down onto it until the
   joint holds its position but still moves easily with two fingers. Give the pitch joint a bit more (it holds
   the grip against gravity).

The **roll joint** is the same idea in line: the grip's M5 runs back through two 685ZZ in the tube on the pitch
link (6 mm spacer tube between them, a spacer ring behind the rear one), the nyloc nut and the roll magnet cap
turn with the grip, and the board slides into its slot from the side and is held by the roll clip.

## Wiring

Each AS5600 module: **VCC → 3.3 V, GND → GND, OUT → an ADC pin on the ESP32-S3, DIR → GND** (fixes the counting
direction; flip the sign in software if a joint reads backwards). 6 encoders + the thumbstick (2 axes; only up /
down is used) + the button = 7 analog inputs + 1 digital. Run the wires along the links and tie them down; leave a
loose loop at each joint for its full range.

## Joint ranges (checked in CAD)

The leader is free at least 5° past every follower limit: shoulder ±115° and elbow ±125° (no contact), wrist pan
±122° (contact only at +130°), pitch −15…+115° (contact at +125°), roll ±120° (no contact), trigger 0–30°.

The links are 14 × 25 mm box sections between the forks; they only get thin (12 mm tall) within 17 mm of the next
joint, where its fork passes above and below.
